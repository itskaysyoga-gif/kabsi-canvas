## Decisions of 21 Oct 2026: what is coming and what is changing

Final product decisions were made on 21 Oct 2026. Most are not built yet. This block says which, so the assistant never sells something that does not exist and never hides something that is ending.

**How to read the markers (assistant rule).**

- **[COMING]** means decided but not built. Say it is **not available yet**, give **no date**, and never offer it as something the person can use or buy now. If someone asks for it, say what exists today first, then that it is planned.
- **[CHANGING]** means true today and being removed or replaced. Describe what is true today. If the person is deciding whether to buy or sign up, say it is changing and that the team will confirm the current offer; never promise it will last.
- When a card is merged, the marker is removed from the line in the same change. A line with no marker is true today.
- The claims rule: never quote an industry statistic (for example a percentage of "more calls" or "ranking") as fact, and never say what Google's algorithm rewards. Say what Kabsi does.

**Holiday hours (live from 21 Oct 2026, P0.5-03).** Five days before a public holiday Kabsi asks the owner by email, and in Kabsi under Hours, what the business will do: **normal hours**, **closed**, or **custom hours**. Kabsi never assumes holiday hours. The email has one button, "Choose your hours", and three plain links, one for each answer. Normal hours records the answer and changes nothing on Google. Custom hours are read back before they are saved. Confirming closed or custom hours needs the owner (or a manager the owner has let approve hours) signed in; normal hours needs only the link. A moon-dependent holiday (Eid, Arafah Day, the Islamic New Year, the Prophet's birthday) says "expected" and the owner can move the days up to three either way. Ramadan is asked once, before the month starts, with two answers: normal hours or different hours for the whole month. The holidays come from each country's own official source, checked on 21 Oct 2026, for England and Wales (GOV.UK bank holidays), the United States (the federal holidays), Canada (the general holidays of the Canada Labour Code), New Zealand, Singapore and the United Arab Emirates. **Australia and Ireland** are different: their official pages could not be read, so Kabsi uses the national public days from a software package (date-holidays), checked on 22 Oct 2026, and says so in its own records; state and county holidays are not covered, and the team checks these two against the official pages when it can. ### 2.1a The five modules

## 8. Google policy and review best practice

### 8.1 How Google local results work

Kabsi does the parts an owner controls: accurate details, a reply to every review, regular updates, and easy reviewing for every customer. It promises no rankings and does not say what Google's ranking uses or rewards.

### 8.2 Replying to reviews (Google's steps)

1. Open your Business Profile (search your business name on Google while signed in, or go to business.google.com).
2. Select **Read reviews**.
3. Next to the review, select the **Reply** icon, write, and select **Reply**.

Google checks every reply against its content policies before it appears, usually within 10 minutes, and Google says it can sometimes take up to 30 days (Google's help, read 5 Nov 2026). The reply shows under the review with the business name, and the customer is notified. You can edit or delete it later on Google.

A good reply: two to four sentences, in the customer's language, mentions one thing they said, no selling or discount codes, signed as the business, promises nothing you won't do.

### 8.3 Negative reviews

1. Wait until you're calm.
2. Thank them for telling you.
3. Name the problem in one sentence, without arguing in public.
4. Ask them to get in touch through the details on your profile. Don't write a phone number, email or link in the reply: Google can reject replies that contain them.
5. Keep it short and sign as the business.

Never: argue about who is right, share the customer's details, accuse them of lying, or offer discounts or refunds for changing or removing the review (Google treats that as fake engagement). Replying promptly is Google's advice.

### 8.4 Removing reviews

- Nobody outside Google can remove a review, whatever they charge. Google removes reviews that break its policies (spam and fake engagement, profanity, harassment or hate speech, off-topic content, personal information), not reviews that are simply negative.
- **Report from your profile:** open your Business Profile, **Read reviews**, select the **Report** icon next to the review, choose the reason, **Send report**.
- **Or use Google's Reviews Management Tool:** sign in with the account that manages the profile, choose the business, **Continue**, **Report a new review for removal**, choose the reason, **Submit**. A decision usually takes several days. Statuses: "Decision pending", "Report reviewed - no policy violation", "Escalated - check your email for updates". If the review stays up, you can appeal once from the same tool.
- While you wait, reply calmly. Help page: support.google.com/business/answer/4596773.

### 8.5 Asking for reviews

- Get your link from your profile (**Read reviews**, then **Get more reviews**, then **Copy**; the QR code is available on a computer browser), or use Kabsi's free tool.
- Ask everyone the same way. Google prohibits offering incentives (free or discounted goods or services) for reviews, and asking only customers you expect to be happy.
- An honest mix of reviews is more convincing to readers anyway.

### Google Protection

**What is Google Protection?**
Kabsi watches your business name, phone, website, address, opening hours, main category, open status (open, temporarily closed, permanently closed) and map pin, and compares them with the details you confirmed. When Google shows something different, you get one email that explains it, for example "Google shows Sunday closing at 18:00. You approved 22:00.", and says whether Google made the change itself or Kabsi does not know who did. On Kabsi you choose: "Google is right" (Google's value becomes your approved detail and nothing is sent to Google) or "Keep my information" (Kabsi sends your approved value back to Google, then checks Google shows it). Nothing changes on Google unless you say so, and Kabsi never puts anything back by itself. A change waiting for the owner appears as a card on Home.

**Can Google Protection lock my Google profile?**
No. Nobody can lock a Google profile or stop people suggesting edits. Kabsi tells you fast and puts your version back only if you say so.

**How fast will I hear about a change?**
Kabsi checks about once an hour once connected, and emails you when a detail changes.

**What if the change was mine?**
Tap "Google is right". Google's value becomes your approved version.

**Why does Kabsi sometimes recommend "Google is right"?**
Google removes things from business names that break its naming rules: a web address, a city or area, a slogan or extra keywords. When that is what Google removed (for example "Rosa Bakery.com" to "Rosa Bakery"), Kabsi recommends "Google is right" and says why: business names on Google can't include them, and putting them back can lead to a suspension. "Keep my information" is still there, with that warning.

**Why do I have to sign in for some changes?**
Your business name, address, main category, open status and map pin can make Google ask you to verify your business again when they change. Kabsi asks you to sign in to choose these (an email link alone is not enough; the email page asks you to sign in and brings you back), only the owner can choose, and Kabsi says "Changing this can make Google ask you to verify your business again." Kabsi also sends at most one of these details back to Google at a time, at least 7 days apart; a second one inside the 7 days waits, and Kabsi says the date it can go.

**What if Google keeps changing the same detail?**
Kabsi puts the same detail back at most twice in 30 days. If Google changes it a third time, Kabsi does not send it again: a person on the Kabsi team contacts Google Business Profile support for you, with an explanation ready.

**What does "Replaced by a newer change" or "No answer in 14 days" mean?**
"Replaced by a newer change": Google changed the same detail again before you chose, so Kabsi shows you the newest change instead; nothing was decided for you. "No answer in 14 days": nobody chose within 14 days, so the change is closed without a decision and Kabsi does not email about that same value again. Nothing was put back or kept for you in either case.

**Which details count as approved by me?**
Only details you confirmed yourself in Kabsi, or where you chose "Google is right". Values Kabsi read from Google are "Not yet confirmed by you" until you confirm them: Kabsi still watches them and tells you when they change, but never offers to put an unconfirmed value back. Everything you saved in About your business counts as confirmed by you.

## 12. Glossary

- **Google Business Profile:** your business's listing on Google Search and Maps.
- **Manager / Owner:** Google profile roles. A Manager can reply, post and edit details; only an Owner can add or remove people or remove the profile. Kabsi is a Manager.
- **People and access:** the Google screen where owners add or remove Managers.
- **hello@kabsi.co:** Kabsi's support email. **5481006796:** Kabsi's Google business group ID (Kabsi Clients), invited as Manager.
- **Reviews:** one screen for every review. Filters: Needs reply, All, Replied, Positive (4 and 5 stars), Neutral (3), Negative (1 and 2) and High risk. Each review shows its stars, text, date, name, risk and one status: Draft ready, Waiting for you, Published, Google is checking your reply, Rejected by Google, or You chose not to reply. On a phone one review fills the screen with **Approve reply** as the large button, then **Edit** and **Other options** (Shorter, More formal, Warmer, Regenerate, I'll reply myself, Skip). An owner can approve; a manager only for what the owner turned on; staff do not see reviews. The older address for "To reply" opens Reviews, Needs reply.
- **Risk level:** low, medium or high, set for each review. Medium shows the issue above the draft. High risk: no draft, a short checklist, and a neutral holding reply only if the owner asks for one.
- **You replied yourself:** a review you chose to answer on Google yourself. Kabsi posts nothing.
- **Daily email:** one email a day that groups what came up since the last one: reviews that came in outside your opening hours with their drafts, profile changes, posts and photos to approve.
- **About your business:** the facts Kabsi may use in drafts.
- **Never-say list:** "Anything Kabsi should never say or promise".
- **Safety check:** the automatic check of each draft against Kabsi's rules and your never-say list.
- **Kabsi's posts:** the posts Kabsi prepares each month (2 by default, 1 or 4 by choice) for your approval.
- **Search phrases:** words people use to search for a business like yours, worked into a post's first line. They come from your category, your area and the services you listed in About your business, and, once Kabsi is connected to Google live, from Google's own search terms. Never from your reviews.
- **Special hours:** hours for specific dates, like holidays.
- **Google Protection:** watches key profile details, alerts you and lets you choose on Kabsi whether to keep your information.
- **Profile health:** the suggestions list on Home, no score.
- **Review link:** go.kabsi.co/{CODE}, opens your Google review page, or a short Kabsi page with the Google button first if you chose that. Up to 5 per business.
- **NTAG213 / NTAG215:** common NFC chip types that work for review links.
- **NFC Tools:** a free app for writing a link onto an NFC tag.
- **Tap / open:** one opening of your review page. Not a review.
- **Card code:** the 6-character code under a Kabsi card's QR.
- **Test mode:** [TIME-SENSITIVE] Kabsi's state until Google approves its API access. Nothing is sent to Google automatically.
- **Early access:** [TIME-SENSITIVE] a person on the Kabsi team does the Google steps by hand and posts what the owner approves, within one working day.
