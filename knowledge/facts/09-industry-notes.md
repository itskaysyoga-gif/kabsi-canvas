## 7. Industry notes

Site examples are fictional and labelled. The same honesty rules apply to every trade. In every trade: ask every customer the same way, and never offer a discount, free item, gift or upgrade for a review.

### 7.1 Restaurants
Reviews land during service; drafts wait in email. Guests write in many languages; each draft matches. Illness and hygiene complaints are high risk: such a review gets no draft: it is marked "This review needs a little more care" with a short checklist, and Kabsi writes a short, neutral holding reply only if the owner asks (13, "What happens with hard reviews?"). Replies mention menu, delivery or reservations only if the owner gave those facts and the guest raised the topic; never invented dishes, prices, offers or discounts. Useful: special hours, dish photos checked, Google Protection for phone and hours. Card: on tables, at the host stand or till, printed QR on the bill folder or takeaway bag.

### 7.2 Cafés and bakeries
Post drafts after the morning rush. The weekly draft suits what's fresh (a seasonal pastry, a new blend). Early opening hours must be exact; set special hours for holidays. Arabic reviews get Arabic replies. Card: by the coffee machine or pastry counter, at the till, QR on cup sleeves or bread bags.

### 7.3 Clinics and dentists (patient privacy)
Drafts stay calm and general: no medical claims, no personal details, no admissions of fault, and they ask the reviewer to get in touch through the details on the profile. In many countries health privacy rules (HIPAA in the US, for example) mean a public reply shouldn't confirm someone is a patient or mention their care. Add "Don't confirm the reviewer is a patient or mention any treatment" under **Anything Kabsi should never say or promise**; drafts and the safety check follow it. Weekly drafts arrive on Tuesday. Card: at reception, QR on appointment cards or aftercare sheets, link in follow-up emails. This is not legal advice; privacy law questions go to a person.

### 7.4 Salons and barbers
Add team names so replies can thank the right stylist; names not on the list are never used. Share photos of the work, not the person: the photo check does not look for faces (what it checks is in the FAQ answer "What photos can I post?"). Card: at each station by the mirror, at reception, link in booking confirmations.

### 7.5 Hotels and B&Bs
Guests review in many languages; each draft matches. Check-in times, parking and breakfast hours come from About your business. Google Protection watches phone, address, website and hours; room photos are checked. Kabsi works with Google only, not booking sites. Card: at reception at check-out, in the room next to the Wi-Fi details, link in the thank-you email after the stay.

### 7.6 Garages and auto repair
Price disputes get a calm draft that asks the customer to get in touch through the details on the profile, with no quick Post button. Drafts never argue, admit fault or promise refunds or discounts. Google Protection matters for the phone number. Card: where customers pay and collect keys, QR on the invoice, link in the "your car is ready" message.

### 7.7 Shops and boutiques
Weekly drafts for new arrivals, with a Shop or Call button; never invented prices, offers or dates. Special hours for holiday openings. Each shop is its own business in Kabsi with its own plan. Card: at the till, QR on receipts or bags, link in order confirmations.

### 7.8 Florists
Drafts are warm for celebrations and gentle for sympathy orders, never adding details the customer didn't share. Special hours for busy seasons. Orders come by phone, so Google Protection watching the number matters. Arrangement photos checked. Card: tucked into each bouquet or delivery, at the counter, link in delivery confirmations.

### 7.9 Lash and brow studios (Industries 2)
Add the artists to the team list so a reply thanks the right one; names not on the list are never used. Share close photos of the work, the lashes and brows, not the client's whole face, and ask first if you like. A review about irritation or a reaction gets no draft: it is marked "This review needs a little more care" with a short checklist, and Kabsi writes a short, neutral holding reply only if the owner asks (13, "What happens with hard reviews?"). Search term for the free check: "eyelash salon".

### 7.10 Tattoo studios (Industries 2)
Say in the description and in posts whether the studio takes walk-ins or works by appointment; Kabsi watches the description and hours every day and tells the owner when Google shows something else. Healed work photos make good posts that name the artist. A review about an infection or another safety problem gets no draft: it is marked "This review needs a little more care" with a short checklist, and Kabsi writes a short, neutral holding reply only if the owner asks (13, "What happens with hard reviews?"); Kabsi never gives medical advice. Search term: "tattoo shop".

### 7.11 Pet groomers (Industries 2)
A groomer who travels to customers sets a service area on Google and can hide an address customers cannot visit; Kabsi watches the service area. A review about a shaved coat gets a careful draft that asks the owner to get in touch and admits nothing; a review about a nick or an injury gets no draft: it is marked "This review needs a little more care" with a short checklist, and Kabsi writes a short, neutral holding reply only if the owner asks (13, "What happens with hard reviews?"). Search term: "pet groomer".

### 7.12 Vet clinics (Industries 2)
Hours and the phone matter most; set special hours for every holiday. Replies thank the owner and say nothing about a pet's diagnosis or treatment; a review about a pet that was hurt or died gets no draft: it is marked "This review needs a little more care" with a short checklist, and Kabsi writes a short, neutral holding reply only if the owner asks (13, "What happens with hard reviews?"). Search term: "veterinarian".

### 7.13 Car washes and detailing (Industries 2)
Open status and hours are checked every day. A photo of a finished car makes a good post (one photo per post). A review that says the car was damaged gets a careful draft that asks the customer to get in touch, never argues, admits fault or promises a refund. Search term: "car wash".

### 7.14 Opticians (Industries 2)
An eye test is health care: replies never mention a result, a prescription or a condition, and invite the patient to get in touch. The booking link (website) and hours are checked every day. Photos of new frames make good posts; Kabsi never writes a price the owner did not give. Search term: "optician".

### 7.15 Yoga and pilates studios (Industries 2)
Address and hours are checked every day, which matters after a move. A new class becomes a post with the real day and time the owner gave. Teachers are thanked by name only from the team list. A review about an injury in class gets no draft: it is marked "This review needs a little more care" with a short checklist, and Kabsi writes a short, neutral holding reply only if the owner asks (13, "What happens with hard reviews?"); Kabsi never gives health advice. Search term: "yoga studio".

Florists follow 7.8; their page is new (search term "florist"), and kabsi.co/for/florists now forwards to it.
