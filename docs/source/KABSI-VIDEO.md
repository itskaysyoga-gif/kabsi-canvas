**Decisions for Rashid.** Each has a default the plan already follows; change it and the affected videos change.

1.  **Target markets changed.** The growth playbook was built around the Gulf and Arabic. This plan targets the US, Canada, UK, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand, Singapore and the UAE in English. There Kabsi loses its Arabic advantage and competes with Birdeye, Podium and Google's own Gemini in Business Profile. Default: English markets, with Kabsi's edge stated as profile watching, approval before anything goes live, the free setup call and price. The growth playbook carries the same change (G-41).

2.  **The NFC card.** The audit made NFC a fulfilment option for the Lebanon bundle and partners only. Selling it in eight countries needs a supplier, a price, shipping and someone to post cards. Default: videos show the printable QR table card every customer gets; NFC appears only as "NFC version available" until fulfilment is decided.

3.  **No real faces.** Nobody from Kabsi appears on camera. The plan uses one disclosed AI presenter and one recurring AI business owner, both built once in Higgsfield (Part 4, "Characters").

4.  **Production happens on the Higgsfield website, by Rashid.** Not through the Claude connector, which spends credits on every call and cannot use Higgsfield's unlimited windows. Rashid's Claude does the one-time setup (demo data, scripts, prompts, brand kit text, booking link, tracking); Rashid then makes each video on higgsfield.ai, downloads the finished file and posts it. Still images come from Gemini or ChatGPT, free.

5.  **The setup call is not a video.** It appears on the website, in onboarding, in emails and in ad text (Conversion kit), and is written into the audit (K-89) and design review.

6.  **No customer proof yet.** No video may show or imply a customer who does not exist (Rules every video must follow). The customer story (#16) waits for a real customer's own video and written consent.

7.  **"Voice commands".** Kabsi has no voice feature today, so no video shows one. Voice-over yes; voice interaction with Nora would be a product decision.

8.  **Comments and DMs.** Saved replies and Meta Business Suite automated answers for common questions; every setup question gets the booking link.

9.  **Tax on foreign sales.** Rashid's Claude decides whether and when VAT or GST matters before ads run in each country.

10. **Tool limits.** Higgsfield changes plans, prices and features often. On day one Rashid checks his plan shows 1,000 credits, which models are in its unlimited window this cycle, and that its editor, captions, voice and brand kit work as described in Part 4. If one finishing step is missing on his plan, that single step is done in CapCut's web editor, and the video is still downloaded ready to post.

## How Rashid uses this plan

Rashid makes every video himself on the Higgsfield website, following the cards in Part 2, and downloads a finished file ready to post on Instagram and Facebook. Images come from Gemini (or ChatGPT) for free; the product screens are short recordings from his phone. Rashid's Claude is used once at the start for setup, then only to write new hooks and read results. Expected time: about 4 hours a week in weeks 1 to 6, about 2 hours after that.

**Rules for every video**

1.  Never generate the Kabsi app, a Google screen or a logo from scratch with AI. Product screens are the real phone recordings from step 3, shown full screen; AI never redraws or animates them.

2.  Never show Google's logo or a copy of Google Maps. Say "Google" in words only. Website videos and ad descriptions carry "Kabsi is not affiliated with Google."

3.  The first 2 seconds carry the hook in picture and on-screen text, because most people watch muted; Kabsi's name and one approved claim appear by the third second. Every video has burned-in captions.

4.  Keep text inside the safe area: on a 1080 x 1920 frame keep Meta's safe zones clear of text, logos and key product screens: about 14% at the top (270 px), 35% at the bottom (672 px) and 6% on each side (65 px).

5.  Every paid creative ships in three cuts: 9:16 for Reels and Feed (10 to 15 s for tests), 9:16 for Stories (under 10 s), and 4:5 (1080 x 1350) for Feed; 1920 x 1080 (16:9) for the website. Longer versions (20 to 35 s) are for organic posts and retargeting only. H.264, 30 fps.

6.  Use only words from the claims list (Rules every video must follow). If a sentence is not on it and is not plainly true today, cut it.

## Conversion kit: hooks, offers and calls to action

Every video gets a hook in its first 2 seconds, one promise, and one call to action matched to how warm the viewer is, and it lands on a page that repeats the same promise. Hooks are what Part 8 tests first, because they move results more than anything else in a video.

**The offer (all true today; use any of it in a CTA)**

- 14 days free, no card. Then \$19 a month.

- A free setup call: someone from Kabsi connects the Google profile with the owner by phone or video call, screen share only, no camera needed. Rashid is available for every customer who wants one; book through Rashid's Calendly link on the site, in emails and in ads.

- Review link, QR code and printable table card included.

- Nothing goes live without the owner's approval; Kabsi can be disconnected any time.

**Hook rules:** open mid-action (the phone buzzing, the review on screen), never on a logo or a greeting; the problem visible in frame 1; one specific detail (a time, a star count, a trade); name the trade in trade-specific ad variants ("Salon owners:"); each original ships with hooks A, B and C so the ad test starts with three openings, not one.

**Make the click convert**

- The landing page headline repeats the ad's hook promise; W1 sits under the first screen; price, "14 days free, no card" and the setup-call link are visible without scrolling on a phone.

- Instagram bio: "Your reviews and listing. Taken care of. 14 days free, no card." with the landing link (UTM-tagged). Story highlights: How it works, Pricing, Free setup call, FAQ. Facebook Page button: Sign up.

- Every Reel caption ends with the CTA and "link in bio"; Stories use a link sticker.

- Comment automation: a comment with "SETUP" gets an automatic DM with the booking link (Meta's automated responses or a tool such as ManyChat).

- Retargeting runs in order: proof (#7) on days 1 to 3, offer (#12) on days 4 to 7, \#3 Meet Kabsi with the setup-call line as ad text ("Not techy? Book a free 15-minute setup call") from day 8.

### The protocol for every clip

1.  Start from an approved still: the shot's final composition, 9:16, 2K, no text, no logos (Part 5). A clip is only as good as its first frame.

2.  Animate with the model from the table: 5 s, 1080p, one output per run.

3.  Motion prompt describes only movement: one camera move and one or two small actions, never the scene again. Pattern: "\[static / slow push-in / slow dolly\]. \[One small action\]. \[One ambient detail\]. Subtle, natural, realistic movement. No text, no logos."

4.  Check faces, hands, morphing and stray letters. Most clips need only 2 to 3 good seconds.

5.  If it fails, change one thing and try once more (less motion, simpler action, static camera). After two failures, fix the still, never keep re-rolling.

## Part 8: Testing, analytics and variations

A creative wins when it brings owners who grant Google access at the lowest cost, not when it gets the most views. Every decision below follows from that, using the growth playbook's numbers: about \$6.80 per trial and about \$11 per access-granted business pay back; during learning, up to three times that is accepted, and any ad set above \$35 per access-granted business after \$300 is cut (G-01). The thresholds marked "starting point" are practice, not sourced benchmarks; recalibrate them on Kabsi's own data after 30 days.

**What to measure, in funnel order**

| Metric                           | How it is calculated               | Good (starting point)                    | Act if                                                         |
|----------------------------------|------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------|
| Hook rate                        | 3-second video plays ÷ impressions | 30% or more                              | Below 20% after 1,500 impressions: pause, re-cut the first 2 s |
| Hold rate                        | ThruPlays ÷ 3-second plays         | 25% or more                              | Below 15%: the middle is slow; cut it shorter                  |
| Link click-through rate          | Link clicks ÷ impressions          | 1% or more                               | Below 0.6% after 2,000 impressions: CTA or offer unclear       |
| Landing page view rate           | Landing page views ÷ link clicks   | 70% or more                              | Below 60%: the page is too slow; fix the page, not the ad      |
| Trial rate                       | Trials ÷ landing page views        | 8% or more                               | Below 4%: the page does not match the ad's promise             |
| Cost per trial                   | Spend ÷ trials                     | \$6.80 target; up to \$20 while learning | Above \$20 after \$40 on one creative: pause                   |
| Access rate                      | Google access granted ÷ trials     | 35% or more                              | Below 25%: onboarding problem; push W3, fix the step           |
| Cost per access-granted business | Spend ÷ access granted             | \$11 target; up to \$35 while learning   | Above \$35 after \$300 on an ad set: cut (G-01)                |
| Trial to paid                    | Paying ÷ trials, after day 14      | 12% or more (audit target)               | Below 9%: product or price problem, not an ad problem          |

**Funnel goals after the click** (product-side, from the Meta-specialist review): 60% of registrations grant Google access within 24 hours (35% is the learning-phase floor above); 80% of access-granted businesses approve their first prepared reply within 72 hours; 20% of access-granted businesses become paying. If registrations are good but access is low, fix onboarding (audit K-91 to K-98, K-111) before buying more traffic; if access is good but payment is low, fix the trial and emails (K-101), not the ads.

**Decision rules for each creative**

- **Too early to judge** until it has 1,500 to 2,000 impressions and 4 to 7 days (whichever is longer) for the hook, and about \$50 to \$75 or 2 to 3 registrations for conversions; at \$20 to \$25 a day that often takes 7 to 14 days, because Meta does not spend evenly across ads. In the first 48 hours check only delivery, rejections and broken renders.

- **Pause** if any "Act if" line on hook rate, click-through or cost per trial is hit, or if it has spent about \$50 to \$75 over at least 7 days with no registration. Replace an ad sooner only if it is wrong, broken or rejected.

- **Winner** if it has at least 5 trials, cost per trial of \$10 or less, an access rate of 35% or more, and beats the median creative in its ad set by at least 30% on cost per access-granted business. With numbers this small, a winner shows a direction, not a proven result: confirm it in a second country before scaling hard.

- **Scale** a winner by raising its ad set budget about 20% every 3 days while cost per access-granted stays under the target band. Never double a budget in one step.

- **Refresh** when frequency passes 2.5 in 7 days in prospecting, or cost per trial rises 50% above the creative's own first-week level.

**Turning a winner into variations.** Each winner gets four variations, built with the re-cut recipe in Part 2, one change each: two new hooks (first 2 s), a different length (15 s or 30 s), and a different format (the same script as a faceless demo or with Leah presenting). Launch them together in the winner's ad set with the original kept running as the control. A variation that beats the control becomes the new control; one that loses after the same spend is paused. Then change the next thing: the trade shown, the country, the offer line.

**Organic signals that promote a video to an ad:** non-follower reach on Instagram Trial Reels, average percentage watched, and saves plus shares per 1,000 accounts reached. Videos in the top quarter of the account on two of these three join the next ad batch.

**Rhythm**

| When                              | Who                                                   | What                                                                                                                   |
|-----------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Tuesday and Friday, 30 min        | Rashid                                                | Apply the pause, winner and refresh rules; nothing else changes mid-week                                               |
| Friday                            | Rashid                                                | Fill the weekly scorecard; pick next week's re-cuts from the winners                                                   |
| First Friday of the month, 45 min | Rashid, with his Claude reading the month's scorecard | What the month taught (message, trade, country), budget level for next month, credit log review                        |
| Day 60                            | Both                                                  | End of the learning phase: keep, change or stop Meta based on cost per access-granted business against the \$11 target |

**Weekly scorecard (Google Sheet), one row per ad per week:** date, ad name, country, spend, impressions, hook rate, hold rate, link click-through, landing page views, trials, cost per trial, access granted, cost per access-granted, decision.

**Check Meta against Kabsi's own data every Friday.** Make sure Kabsi saves the UTM tags with each sign-up (add it if it does not yet), so trials and Google access per ad can be counted in Kabsi's database and PostHog. Where Meta and Kabsi disagree, decide on Kabsi's numbers; Meta's modelled conversions are useful for direction only.

## Rules every video must follow

Every script, caption and ad text must follow these rules. The scripts in this plan are written to them, but before any video runs as an ad it passes the feature truth check below, and any line that describes a feature not yet live is cut or the video stays organic. A video that breaks one is not published, however good it looks.

**Claims**

| Say                                                                                            | Never say                                                                                                      |
|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| "Kabsi drafts replies to your Google reviews. You approve."                                    | "Automatically replies", "posts for you automatically"                                                         |
| "Nothing is published without your approval."                                                  | "Real-time", "24/7 monitoring", "Google Protection" (until Google approves API access and the feature is live) |
| "Kabsi checks your Google profile and tells you when something changes."                       | "Rank higher", "get to \#1 on Google", "guaranteed more reviews", "boost your rating"                          |
| "One short report every week."                                                                 | Any score or grade for a profile                                                                               |
| "\$19 a month. 14 days free, no card."                                                         | "Official Google partner", "approved by Google", "Google-certified"                                            |
| "Google says more reviews and good ratings can help local ranking" (with the source on screen) | Any statistic not from Kabsi's own data (G-40); "trusted by X businesses" until it is true and countable       |

**Approved brand wording** (always allowed): "Your reviews and listing. Taken care of.", "Kabsi looks after your business on Google: a reply ready for every new review, your hours and details kept right, and fresh posts. You just approve." (audit K-110).

**Feature truth check before any paid use.** Every card lists the features its video shows (profile alerts, holiday reminders, risky-review flags, email approval, Nora, the weekly report, the review card). Before a video runs as an ad, Rashid's Claude confirms each one works in production for a new customer; a video showing a feature that is not live stays organic-only, with the "Early access" tag. Also never use: superlatives ("easiest", "best"), speed claims ("in seconds", "one tap") unless measured, or generalisations about owners or customers.

**Reviews (Google's rules and the audit)**

- Never show or suggest asking only happy customers, rewards or discounts for reviews, "leave us 5 stars", staff names on cards, or a customer using the shop's phone or tablet. Show customers using their own phone.

- Never show a real customer's review in marketing without that reviewer's and the business's written permission. Screen recordings use the demo workspace and carry a small "Demo data" tag in a corner.

**People and AI**

- No fake customers or testimonials. The US FTC rule in force since October 2024 bans testimonials that misrepresent who the person is or their experience, and it explicitly covers AI-generated ones. AI people in this plan are scene actors ("a café owner") or a disclosed presenter, never someone who says they use Kabsi.

- Any ad with an AI presenter says "Presenter is AI-generated" in the video and the ad text, and uses Meta's AI label if offered.

- Leah is always Kabsi's AI presenter: she never claims to be a customer, an owner or the founder. Anyone connected to Kabsi (team, friends, partners, related businesses) is disclosed as such if they ever appear.

- Real people on camera sign a simple release covering social media, the website and ads.

<!-- -->

- In paid versions, AI scene actors carry a small "Illustrative scene" tag, so a scene is never mistaken for a real customer.

- Partner ads (#13) run only in the partner campaign once the programme is live, and the landing page shows the full terms: commission, duration, payout conditions.

- No unsupported comparisons or generalisations ("doing more selling than your website", "most owners never...", "probably wrong", "less than a coffee"), no time-saved figures without Kabsi's own measured data, and no wording that asserts something about the viewer's own business ("your hours just changed").

**Google's brand**

- Say "Google" in words; never show Google's logo, Maps pins, or a recreated Google interface in ads. Google's screens appear only where they teach (the onboarding guide, W3, guides and the organic "Google tips" Reels in Part 2), unaltered except for a highlight, and never in ads or boosted posts.

- "Kabsi is not affiliated with Google." under website videos and in ad descriptions where space allows.

**Before Google approves API access (Gate A)**

- Organic videos may show the product as designed, with a small "Early access" tag on screen, and end with "Join early access" or "Free Profile Check at kabsi.co" instead of "Start free".

- No paid ads ("Read first", gate 1).

**Music and fonts:** only Meta Sound Collection or a paid licence that covers ads; never trending tracks in ads. Lalezar and Readex Pro are open-licence fonts and safe to use.
