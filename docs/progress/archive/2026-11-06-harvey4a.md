## What each part does

- **A.** `outbound_settings` (one row; a trigger refuses opening sending or clearing a stop to anyone but postgres, that is a lane R migration), `outbound_prospects`, `outbound_messages`, `outbound_events`, `outbound_suppression` (sha256 of a lower-cased address or domain; address, domain and parent domains are looked up, Harvey's rule), `outbound_days` (tallies that survive deletions). Staff read through RLS (`is_staff`); `staff_outbound_approve` (Email 1 approves its follow-up), `_approve_all` (only the ids shown), `_edit` (no dashes, no exclamation marks, only kabsi.co links), `_skip`. `outbound_refresh_suppression()` adds every Kabsi user, partner account and contact (address, and own domain unless free mail). Views `outbound_dashboard_counts` (exactly the 21 agreed columns) and `outbound_dashboard_days` (day, sent, replies), service role only, numbers and dates only.
- **B.** Weekdays 05:00 UTC. Reads `data/register.csv` (GitHub contents API, raw). GB: registered company, company number, Companies House status active, email on the business's own domain. Salons and barbers first. One fetch of the email's source page (robots.txt, 10 s, 1 MB), the address must still be on it, the first true detail, the templates word for word; no detail, no draft. Test mode: three fictional example.com businesses to the test addresses.
- **C.** Phone first: lock state read only, the drafts with source page, date, detail and company number, Approve, Edit, Edit follow-up, Skip, Approve all after the list, Replies with the prepared answer (Send, Edit, Skip) or Done. No control opens sending.
- **D.** Every 10 minutes, at most one message a tick: lock and test addresses, the window in the recipient's time zone, the ramp from `started_on`, the monthly cap, suppression again right before sending, links and house style again. A message is claimed first and recorded as sent the moment Zoho accepts it; a claimed message never recorded is marked failed after 30 minutes for a person, never re-sent. The next slot is taken atomically (20 minutes plus up to 10 of jitter). Plain text, a real Message-ID, threading for the follow-up, mailto List-Unsubscribe.
- **F.** `outbound_check_stops`: any complaint, or bounces above 3 percent of the last 100 sends (from the tallies), stop and alert. Daily retention: written to with no reply, 90 days after the last email, deleted and its domain coded with reason contacted; a no or a bounce keeps only the hash; a draft never sent is deleted after 30 days; a yes and a reply nobody has handled are kept.
- **E.** Every 10 minutes, read only (EXAMINE, BODY.PEEK), from the last UID. Matches by In-Reply-To and References, a bounce by the headers it returns, and an unsubscribe click by its sender. Harvey's English and Spanish opt-out rules ported with their tests (Hebrew not ported: Outbound writes in English). Every step is done before the event is recorded, so a failure is repeated next run; the complaint stop comes first; GitHub gets the hashes through a queue that waits on any error. The staff alert "New reply to Kabsi outbound" carries no business detail. A clear yes runs the free check inside places_daily_cap (test businesses get a fixed test code and spend nothing) and drafts Rashid's reply.

## Independent review (parts D, E and F)

A fresh agent given only the diff and the rules found 12 problems; all were fixed before merge:
1. A List-Unsubscribe click has no thread headers: now matched by its sender and honoured.
2. A delivered email could be sent again: claim first, record on acceptance, read timeouts on SMTP and IMAP, an atomic slot.
3. A failed step was never retried and a GitHub error could block a complaint stop: stop first, record last, GitHub through a queue.
4. The bounce rate went blind after deletions: it now reads the tallies.
5. The inbox stopped being read after a stop: it now reads whatever the lock says.
6. Header injection through decoded Message-IDs: IDs are never decoded and kept as tokens; no header may hold a line break.
7. The Harvey file could be overwritten past 1 MB: the file must read back whole or nothing is written.
8. Delay notices counted as bounces: a report must say Action: failed.
9. Complaint and out-of-office false positives: whole phrases only; wording alone is not an auto-reply.
10. Real businesses waiting could block test messages: test mode reads test businesses only.
11. Retention too wide: drafts approved and waiting, and unhandled replies, are kept.
12. Kabsi's own mail could read as a reply: skipped.

CodeQL on PR 455 raised one high alert; the regex patterns on fetched pages were replaced (a plain scan for script and style blocks, robots.txt rules matched as text, a linear hours pattern).
